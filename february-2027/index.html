The practical problem

Where does the work stall?

A job can lose time while a crew waits for equipment, fieldwork waits for a decision, or the same people are spread across several jobs. The course uses forestry cases to examine those delays and decide which changes will make the work move more reliably.

Bring a project you know. Map the work that governs its finish date, decide where to put a buffer, and choose one change you can try when you start the next job.

Who it’s for: Foresters, logging contractors, mill and procurement staff, land managers, and others who coordinate forestry work.

February 2027

Four Monday sessions

Led by Steve Bick, CF, The Forest Business School.

Participants can also arrange one-on-one time with Steve Bick, CF, to talk through a project and apply the course ideas to their own work.

  1. Feb 1

    Project flow and tempo

    See where forestry projects slow down and identify one practical change that could improve the pace of work.

  2. Feb 8

    Critical Chain Project Management

    Identify the sequence of work that controls the finish date, including competition for shared people or equipment. Use buffers to protect the schedule.

  3. Feb 15

    Goldratt’s Rules of Flow

    Limit work in process, reduce multitasking, and get the people, information, and materials in place before work begins.

  4. Feb 22

    Adopting the Critical Chain mindset

    Plan how to introduce the approach, gain cooperation, and sustain improvements in day-to-day work.
